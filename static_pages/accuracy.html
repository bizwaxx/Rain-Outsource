How accurate is this?

Rain-Outsource odds are an estimate. They come from the forecast and rain history, not from the league or the track. Always check the official status first. When a league, venue or track has posted a call, that call wins. Our answers show it first.

The short answer

We tested the odds on 154 real past events in Texas and nearby: games and races where we know what happened. 117 went ahead and 37 did not.

  • 85% of the time, the odds pointed the right way. "Right way" means 50% or more for events that went ahead, and under 50% for events that didn't.
  • We call an answer "confident" when it says 80% or more (it will happen) or 20% or less (it won't).
  • Weather alone never goes below 21%, so every confident answer was a confident "it will happen".
  • Of 39 confident "it will happen" answers, 37 were right and 2 were wrong.
  • Answers from 70% to 79% (74 events) were right 85% of the time.

These are "held out" results. Each event was scored with settings chosen without that event, so the test can't just memorize the answers.

The 2 confident misses

  1. A University of Texas baseball game, April 23, 2023. The game was moved a day early because of a storm forecast. The weather data we use doesn't show that threat: a 5% rain chance, and no storms. We said it would be played, and it wasn't.
  2. A Texas State baseball game, February 19, 2019. It was a cold, wet day: about 43°F, a little rain that morning, and a 5% chance during the game. It was called off. We tested 25 more cold, wet early-season college games. All 16 that were above freezing went ahead, so on days like this games almost always play.

Why we didn't hide them

We could make both misses disappear with one rule: never say more than 79% until the official status is posted. But our answers of 80% or more were right 37 out of 39 times (95%). Capping them would make the odds look worse than they really are, just to pass our own test. So we left that rule off, and we show the misses here instead.

How we picked the events

  • Weather first, outcomes after. Most events were picked by their weather (wet days and dry days, chosen by a fixed rule) before we looked at whether they happened. Then we read the result from a public source: the team, the league, the track or ESPN.
  • Some early events were found through rainout posts. That leans those groups toward cancellations.
  • Events we couldn't verify were left out, and the reasons are listed in our test data.

A caveat

We tried about 43 different settings for our odds caps before picking the ones we use. Picking the best of many makes the test look a little better than real life will be, so read 85% as "about 85%, maybe a bit less."

Bottom line

Use the odds to plan, not to decide. The official status always comes first. If there's no official word yet, check the league, venue or track again before you leave.
