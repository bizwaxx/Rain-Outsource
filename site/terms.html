Terms of use

Draft dated October 20, 2026.

A free service

Rain-Outsource is a free public service from JEEZ Labs. You can use the API and MCP server without a key or an account. We may set fair-use rate limits so it stays up for everyone. The source code is open under the MIT licence.

It's an estimate, not a promise

Play odds and rain chances are estimates. They can be wrong. The league, venue, track or event organizer always has the final say on whether something happens. When we can read their official status, we show it first. When we can't, we say so.

Not safety or engineering advice

Don't use Rain-Outsource for safety decisions, like lightning, flooding, heat or severe weather. It isn't safety, medical, engineering or emergency advice, and it isn't an official National Weather Service product. For safety, follow your local weather service, official warnings and the venue's rules. Construction or work decisions need a qualified person on site.

As is

The service is provided "as is", with no warranty and no uptime guarantee. To the extent the law allows, JEEZ Labs isn't liable for losses from using or relying on it, such as a trip, booking or cancelled plan.

Fair use

  • Don't overload the service, get around its rate limits or try to break it.
  • Only send rainout reports that are true, and only with the person's OK.
  • Don't put personal information in report notes.

Data sources and licences

  • US forecasts: National Weather Service API (US government work).
  • Rain history: NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information.
  • Worldwide forecasts, fallback and place search: Open-Meteo.com, licensed CC BY 4.0. Credit Open-Meteo if you republish this data.
  • Venue and track locations: © OpenStreetMap contributors, under the Open Database License (ODbL). If you republish venue data, keep this credit and the ODbL terms.
  • Official status comes from public league, venue and track pages, each under its owner's terms.

Changes

We may change these terms or the service. Changes will be in the public GitHub history. If paid plans are added, they'll come with their own terms, and the free tier stays free.
